Research

Working papers & projects

I study authoritarian politics and nationalism in developing countries, focusing on relations among political elites and the state’s role in shaping national identity.

Authoritarian Politics

I study power sharing and exclusion among political elites in authoritarian regimes. My research asks how rulers and other elites negotiate access to power, how rivals are excluded from ruling coalitions, and how these processes shape the consolidation of power.

My current projects examine failed coups and the erosion of power sharing, as well as power consolidation after rebel victory.

Nationalism

I study how states shape national identity. I am particularly interested in how education and official narratives define national belonging and contribute to nation building.

My current work examines narratives of national unity and frontier governance in China’s Gaokao, alongside research on shared ethnic identity with political leaders and democratic support.
